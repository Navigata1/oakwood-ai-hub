Executive Read

The original plan is useful. It just needs a stronger spine.

The student/professor materials already identify the right broad categories. The advisory move is not replacement — it is prioritization, technical strengthening, and order discipline.

“The lab should earn its name the moment someone walks in — but every item still has to map to a workflow, a student output, or a defensible procurement reason.”
Tuesday Order Decision Matrix

Order with confidence, not panic.

If an order has to begin immediately, separate safe purchases from layout-sensitive or facility-sensitive decisions.

Safe to begin

Order-safe now

  • Core AI-capable showcase compute / first production workstation, once final SKU is confirmed.
  • Teaching/demo display if placement is obvious and compatible with the room.
  • Safety and compliance basics for soldering, resin, CNC, and electronics.
  • Whiteboards/collaboration surfaces if wall placement is obvious.
  • Professional-services/procurement planning line so the rest of the spend is guided.
Measure first

Hold until walkthrough

  • Desk count, desk dimensions, seating count, and final furniture package.
  • Exact outlet count, data-drop count, and dedicated-circuit requirements.
  • Rack/NAS/server placement and airflow decisions.
  • Raised-floor feasibility, under-floor cabling, and any room-merge concept.
Upgrade / reframe

Do not buy weak

  • Do not treat “dual monitors” as AI capability; displays and compute must be separate lines.
  • Do not buy networking hardware without a topology.
  • Do not underfund cybersecurity virtualization if cyber is part of the lab identity.
  • Do not let maker equipment crowd out the AI production core.
Phase 2

Defer if needed

  • Large resin printing / CNC expansion if it displaces AI compute.
  • Major room merge or structural changes until grant/facility rules are confirmed.
  • Raised-floor implementation if it cannot be approved under the current grant window.
  • Enterprise routers/switches not tied to a required lab topology.

Fee framing

A professional-services line around 10% of gross project is defendable for design, scoping, procurement logic, vendor/facilities coordination, and implementation oversight. On a $70K job, that is about $7K; on a $100K job, about $10K.

Advisories + Recommendations

Build the lab as a production studio.

This is the recommendation layer to carry into the room before sharing any updated page with Dr. Smith.

01 · AI core first

The lab identity should be anchored by AI-capable systems, shared model access, student project pipelines, and visible outputs.

02 · Infrastructure second

Power, cabling, networking, storage, and furniture should be planned as one system. The clean room experience is part of the value.

03 · Maker as module

Robotics, CNC, soldering, resin, and 3D printing are valuable — but they should support the AI lab, not swallow its budget.

04 · Outputs weekly

Success is not equipment ownership. Success is students shipping demos, tools, automations, reports, and solutions that can be shown.

Walkthrough Capture

Leave with constraints, not guesses.

The walkthrough should produce the facts needed to turn the advisory into a procurement memo and room layout.

Measure the room

Length, width, ceiling height, windows, doors, board/display walls, existing desk count, and possible camera/photo angles.

Map power + data

Outlet locations, existing circuits, breaker constraints, network drops, Wi-Fi strength, switch closet access, and dedicated 20A needs.

Confirm grant rules

Can funds cover structural changes, electrical, raised flooring, furniture, software, services, installation, or only equipment/supplies?

Listen to faculty

Ask which workflows matter most: AI/data science, cybersecurity, robotics, electronics, fabrication, teaching display, or student showcase.

Set next deadline

Leave with a commitment: procurement memo + layout recommendation + order-safe list + hold/defer list.

Meeting Language

Collaborative, not overpowering.

Use this frame with Dr. Smith or professors if the existing documents come up.

“I looked at the existing student and faculty work as the starting point. My goal is not to replace what was done, but to make sure the final purchase plan actually supports the lab outcomes Dr. Smith is aiming for.”
