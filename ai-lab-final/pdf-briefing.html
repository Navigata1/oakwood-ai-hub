01
Executive Thesis

The lab is a production studio, not a row of upgraded machines.

The center of the proposal is a student outcome: every participant leaves with visible artifacts that can be shown to faculty, recruiters, grant reviewers, and community partners.

Core Position

Build the room around shipping.

The lab should be designed for weekly progress: app prototypes, local model demos, responsible-AI artifacts, short demo videos, and public showcases. Hardware matters because it makes that cadence real.

Decision Frame

Choose the smallest tier that can produce proof.

The $50K Applied Builder path is the most balanced starting point. The $20K pod proves the thesis faster, while the $100K and $200K paths support research, recruitment, and grant visibility from launch.

UseAI fluency, research, analysis, coding, and presentation.
BuildApps, agents, automations, dashboards, voice, and vision tools.
GovernEvaluation, data handling, approval gates, audits, and policy drafts.

Meeting objective. Confirm the available funding range, the room constraints, the program format, the Year 1 faculty lead, and the first measurable definition of success. Those five answers determine the next procurement memo and pilot curriculum.

Implementation Architecture

The lab should operate as a simple, teachable system.

Student workstations handle daily creation. Shared nodes run larger local models. Cloud credits cover overflow. Storage, backup, and policy make the work repeatable.

Operating Stack

Build: GitHub, VS Code/Cursor-class editors, Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, containers, Vercel, Cloudflare, Supabase or equivalent.

Model work: Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM, Hugging Face, embeddings, RAG, evaluation harnesses, and model cards.

Governance: dataset labels, project briefs, approval gates, test logs, prompt-injection checks, and demo review.

Publication: demo wall, short videos, one-page technical briefs, and portfolio pages.

Launch Rhythm

Faculty bridge: one week before cohort launch, focused on vocabulary, tools, classroom boundaries, and evaluation rubrics.

Student cohort: first cohort selected with a transparent rubric across class years and backgrounds.

Peer mentors: students help operate the lab by month three.

Weekly ritual: build review, responsible-AI check, demo recording, and next sprint plan.

Recommended default. Local-first for student and campus-sensitive work; cloud-burst for experiments that exceed the local memory or training budget. This keeps costs predictable while still giving students modern cloud fluency.

Governance & Measurement

Responsibility is part of the product students learn to build.

The lab should make responsible AI visible in the workflow: every project has a data posture, evaluation plan, human review point, and record of what changed.

Governance Practices
  • Classify datasets before they touch a model.
  • Keep human approval for sensitive actions such as email, database writes, payments, and publication.
  • Run hallucination, bias, safety, and prompt-injection checks before demos.
  • Document model choice, known limits, data sources, and evaluation results.
  • Create a student-data handling policy before the first cohort begins.
Success Metrics
  • Number of shipped student artifacts per cohort.
  • Demo-day attendance and partner follow-up.
  • Internship and portfolio outcomes that cite the lab.
  • Faculty confidence before and after the bridge.
  • Grant, sponsor, and partnership leads created from the pilot.
PolicyLab use policy, data taxonomy, approval gates, and incident workflow.
ArtifactsModel cards, evaluation notes, demo scripts, technical briefs, and repositories.
ReportingA semester dashboard that turns activity into follow-on funding evidence.

Why this matters at an HBCU. Oakwood graduates will build and evaluate systems that affect health, education, finance, civic services, and community life. Teaching governance as a craft is both a competitive advantage and a moral one.

Meeting Questions & Decision Points

Leave the conversation with enough clarity to write the next memo.

The meeting should narrow uncertainty. The strongest close is not a broad promise; it is a dated next deliverable tied to the tier and format Dr. Smith prefers.

Five Questions To Confirm
  1. What funding range is available, what source funds it, and is there a use-by date?
  2. Which room is likely, and what are its power, network, HVAC, furniture, and security constraints?
  3. What format should the lab support first: course, certificate, club, summer bridge, or faculty development?
  4. Who will provide Year 1 faculty oversight, and who could become a second campus champion?
  5. How should success be measured after the first semester?
Decision Points
  • Pick a target tier or request a room-and-procurement diagnostic.
  • Choose whether the first cohort is credit-bearing or pilot-based.
  • Decide how much local AI is required on day one.
  • Approve a governance baseline before student projects begin.
  • Set the next deliverable date before the meeting ends.
If she asks...Useful answer
Can we start smaller?Yes. The $20K Starter Pod proves the model in one semester, then demo-day proof supports the $50K or $100K expansion.
Why not only cloud?Cloud is useful for overflow. Local AI teaches privacy-bounded handling, predictable inference costs, and real model operations.
Who maintains this?Use standard tools, vendor support, device management, backups, and a student peer-mentor model by month three.
